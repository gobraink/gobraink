Student Counselling Page

Setting Academic Goals

  • Target: Academic goals give your studies clear direction. Focus on achievements and milestones that can be measured.
  • Specific: Pick an exact target instead of a vague idea.
  • Measurable: Choose a goal you can track.
  • Achievable: Make sure the goal is challenging but still possible to reach with hard work.
  • Relevant: Pick goals that matter to the student's personal learning and future.
  • Time-bound: Set a clear deadline or end date for the goal.
  • Raise a grade: Pick one tough subject and work to boost your score by the end of the term.
  • Submission of assignment: Submit all assignments at least one day before the deadline.
  • Strong conception: Clear difficult concepts by asking questions to the teacher during every class.

Rules for Effective Studying

  • Use recall: Look away after reading a page and try to recall the main ideas instead of passively rereading.
  • Test yourself: Use flashcards and quiz yourself on everything to build mental retrieval strength.
  • Chunk your problems: Break down complex concepts into small, easily understandable pieces and practice them until they become second nature.
  • Space your repetition: Spread your study sessions out over several days rather than cramming all at once.
  • Alternate techniques: Mix up different problem-solving methods during your practice sessions instead of repeating the same routine.
  • Take breaks: Step away when you feel frustrated or tired to let your brain process information in the background.
  • Use explanations and analogies: Explain concepts in simple terms or relate them to everyday things to deepen understanding.
  • Collaborate with Study Groups: Studying in groups is very helpful for reinforcing ideas and learning from each other.

Manage Your Time and Take Care of Yourself

  • Write things down: Use a calendar or planner for every test and deadline.
  • Go to class: Showing up is half the battle and saves time later.
  • Stop procrastination: Break big tasks into tiny steps to make starting easier.
  • Sleep well: Pick one tough subject and work to boost your score by the end of the term.
  • Get enough sleep: Pick one tough subject and work to boost your score by the end of the term.
  • Ask for help: Talk to teachers or advisors when you feel stuck.
  • Stay balanced: Make time for friends, hobbies, and exercise.
  • Time tracking: Use a timer to stay on track and avoid getting tired.

Building Daily Habits and Mental Health

  • Use a planner: Write down all test dates and project deadlines the day you get the syllabus.
  • Break big tasks down: Don't wait for motivation; do just ten minutes of a large assignment to get started.
  • Set clear goals: Give your study sessions a specific finish line rather than studying for an open-ended number of hours.
  • Don't compare yourself: Everyone learns at a different speed. Focus only on your own progress.
  • Let go of drama: School social circles change fast. Focus on a few good friends rather than trying to please everyone.
  • Away from Phone: Put your phone away in another room while studying.
  • Review and Revise Regularly: Review notes of every subject for 10 minutes at the end of every evening.
  • Grades aren't everything: Doing your best is what matters. A single bad test will not ruin your future.
